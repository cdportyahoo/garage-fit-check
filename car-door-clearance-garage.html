CAR DOOR CLEARANCE

How much space do you need to open car doors in a garage?

A car can fit between the walls and still leave you struggling to get out. The useful clearance is the space your own door and passengers need—not just the inches left beside a closed vehicle.

Start with your door, not a universal number

There isn’t one gap that works for every vehicle and person. Door length, opening position, seating height and how you get in and out all change the space you need. Getting a child into a car seat or using a mobility aid may need a different arrangement.

Measure the doors you actually use. Opening one just far enough to squeeze out is different from opening it comfortably for daily use.

Measure the opening space in four steps

  1. Park in a clear, level area. Use a place away from traffic, with the vehicle secured against movement and plenty of empty space beside it.
  2. Choose a closed-vehicle reference. For this door check, use the side of the closed vehicle body at that door. Note mirrors separately; don’t mix a body-width measurement with an over-mirror width.
  3. Open the door to your useful position. Try getting in and out as you normally would. Measure the sideways distance, perpendicular to the car, from your reference to the outermost point reached by the door through that opening movement. A helper and removable floor marks can make this easier.
  4. Record the usable gap you want. Leave extra room so the door does not finish against an obstacle. Repeat for other doors and passengers. Check mirrors, getting out, and the route around the car separately.

Measure in open space first. Don’t test the limit by swinging a door toward a cabinet, wall or another car. A tape mark shows a proposed boundary; it does not protect paintwork.

Check the gap beside a cabinet

Measure the shortest clear distance from the parked car to the nearest cabinet face, handle, wall or other obstruction. Compare that available gap with the opening space you measured. Check the heights where the door and mirror pass, too.

ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE

You measure a 26-inch available gap beside the car. Your chosen door position projects 29 inches beyond the same closed-body reference.

26 − 29 = −3 inches

The opening space is 3 inches short, even before adding extra room. Moving the cabinet or car can help; a shallower cabinet may help if its other dimensions and location work.

26 and 29 inches are invented example measurements, not recommended clearances or vehicle specifications.

A cabinet’s doors and drawers need room as well. Check their open dimensions and whether you can stand in front of them with the car parked.

Two cars need a shared-space check

Measure the space between the actual parked vehicles, including mirrors and projecting trim. Check each door people use. A gap that works for one door may not work when doors on both cars are opened together.

If you need simultaneous access, test the combined arrangement with the vehicles stationary and doors kept clear of each other. Consider passenger loading, child seats and the route to the house door. The total spare garage width alone cannot answer those questions.

Use your measurements in the planner

  1. Enter the usable inside garage size and your actual vehicle footprint. Include mirrors in the vehicle width if that is the footprint you want to reserve.
  2. Place the cabinets and other vehicles. Check the displayed clearances and the FIT, REVISE or STOP message.
  3. Set “Door swing gap (in)” under “Your garage” to your chosen planning allowance. If the vehicle width includes mirrors, account for that different reference before transferring a door measurement taken from the body.
  4. Compare arrangements, then verify the door movement and everyday access in the real garage before buying.

The yellow shading shows your chosen gap allowance. Actual door swings aren’t calculated. The same setting applies around the vehicle, including front and rear; it is not a separate measurement for every door. FIT does not confirm that doors open or that a route is accessible.

Before you buy storage

Mark its full footprint, including projecting handles, on the floor with suitable removable tape. Compare your measured door-opening space and check cabinet doors and drawers. Check the walking route separately: door clearance is not a substitute for an access check.
