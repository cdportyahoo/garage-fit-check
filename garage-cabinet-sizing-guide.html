THE CABINET SIZING GUIDE

Storage that fits.
Room to use it.

Start with the car and the space you need beside it. Then work out how much cabinet you can place along the wall.

1. Find your available cabinet depth

Park where you actually want the vehicle. Measure from the wall to the nearest part of the vehicle along the proposed cabinet run, including mirrors or other projections. Use the smallest measurement along that run.

Available cabinet depth = wall-to-car distance − space you want to keep clear

For example, if you measure 54 inches from wall to car and choose to keep 30 inches clear, the remaining footprint depth is 24 inches. A 24-inch cabinet leaves no allowance for handles, wall offsets or measurement error; include those in its footprint or choose a shallower unit.

These numbers demonstrate the calculation. They are not a recommended aisle width or an accessibility standard. Test the space you need for your own use.

2. Work out the usable wall run

Measure the wall section available for storage. Leave out steps, columns and any area needed to operate doors or reach equipment. Check the exact product dimensions, including end panels and installation clearances.

Example runCalculation
Clear wall section100 inches
Three cabinet units, 30 inches wide each3 × 30 = 90 inches
Remaining space100 − 90 = 10 inches

That remaining 10 inches must cover any required side clearances, fillers and offsets. A width calculation alone does not confirm that the products can be installed there.

3. Compare two ways to park

In the planner, start with the car centered. Add a cabinet using its full floor footprint. Then move the car toward one side and compare the room gained at the storage wall with the space lost on the other side.

4. Tape it out before you buy

Mark the cabinet footprints on the actual floor with removable tape suitable for the surface. Park the car in the proposed position and test getting in and out, opening drawers and reaching what you use.

Check cabinet height, overhead garage-door travel, anchoring and loading requirements separately. The planner checks simple top-down rectangles; it does not simulate door swings, overhead objects or a continuous walking route.

A FIT result means the simplified footprints meet the gap you entered. Confirm the real arrangement with your measurements and the product's installation instructions.

Your buying checklist

  • Full cabinet width and depth, including projections.
  • Space to open every door and drawer you need.
  • Required installation gaps and wall offsets.
  • Usable height and garage-door clearance.
  • A saved plan and PDF with the final measurements.
