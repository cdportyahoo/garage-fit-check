PICKUP TRUCK GARAGE PLANNING

Will a pickup truck fit in a 20-foot garage?

Some will. Others are too long, and a truck that barely fits may leave too little room for storage or everyday access. Start with the actual inside depth and your exact truck.

First, make sure “20 feet” means usable inside depth

A nominal garage size may describe the building rather than the parking space. Measure from the inside face of the closed garage door to the nearest obstruction in the truck’s path. Check at bumper height as well as floor level; a shelf or cabinet may project farther than the wall.

A true 20-foot interior is 240 inches. The calculations below assume straight parking with the tailgate closed, no added accessories, and no storage projecting into that depth.

Total length allowance = usable inside depth − actual truck length

That allowance is shared between the front and rear. Eight inches total does not mean eight inches at each end, and a positive result alone does not establish a usable parking arrangement.

What happens when you add cabinets?

Use an illustrative 232-inch truck. In a 240-inch interior it leaves 8 inches total. A cabinet projecting 18 inches from the wall into the truck’s parking path changes the calculation:

240 − 18 − 232 = −10 inches

The truck is now 10 inches too long for that straight parking path, even before allowing any gap at the bumpers. Moving storage to a side wall may preserve depth, but reduces the space available for mirrors, doors and walking.

Length is only the first check

  • Width and mirrors: measure the clear opening and the truck with the mirrors in the position used while entering. Check room to open the driver’s door after parking.
  • Height: measure the truck with its rack, canopy or other accessories and compare it with the lowest obstruction along the entry path, including the open garage door and opener.
  • Attached equipment: include a hitch, carrier, brush guard or anything else projecting past the measured body.
  • Daily access: check the route to the house door, stairs and storage. Opening the tailgate requires additional space beyond the closed-tailgate calculation.

Do not assume diagonal parking solves a length problem: it also uses width and changes the entry route. A parked footprint does not establish that you can maneuver into place.

Before buying the truck or storage

Measure the specific vehicle and garage, then mark any proposed cabinet footprint with removable tape suitable for the floor. If a controlled test fit is practical, check the parked position, door travel and access routes with another person helping you observe clearances. Keep people clear of moving vehicles and doors.
