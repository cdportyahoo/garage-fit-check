THE MEASURING GUIDE

Measure once.
Make space that works.

Before you buy cabinets or shelving, find out how much of your garage you can actually use.

1. Measure the usable floor

Use a tape measure and record everything in inches. Measure the inside width from left to right and depth from the front wall to the garage-door end.

Account for columns, steps, wall projections and items that must stay. An overall room rectangle does not represent every obstruction. Check the door opening separately from the interior width.

2. Measure the car you park

Measure its actual width and length, including anything that sticks out in the way you normally park it. Do not assume a manufacturer's body-width figure includes mirrors.

With the car parked, open the doors, trunk or hatch you need to use. Measure the space those movements need and consider room for people to get in and out. Enter the vehicle footprint in the planner; test these additional movements in the real garage.

3. Check the whole cabinet footprint

Record each cabinet or shelf's width and depth. Include handles, protruding tops and any required offset from the wall. Check the space needed to open cabinet doors and drawers.

The planner uses a top-down footprint. It does not check height, overhead door travel, ceiling storage, wall anchoring or loading limits.

4. Choose a gap that suits your use

Decide how much space you need around the parked car for your own access and activities, then enter that as your desired gap. Try moving the car toward one side if you want more room for a work area.

FIT means the simplified layout meets the gap you entered. It does not certify a usable walking route: the nearest cabinet measurement can run diagonally between corners. Check actual door swings, access and work space on site.

Before buying: your final check

  • Measure the room, car and exact storage products.
  • Try parking and using the doors in the proposed position.
  • Check access to equipment, switches and anything that must remain reachable.
  • Compare the product's installation requirements with the actual location.
  • Save your layout and keep a PDF with every cabinet's dimensions.
